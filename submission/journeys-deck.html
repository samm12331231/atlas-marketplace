The spine

Six stages, and why one of them is separate

Each stage answers one question. The stage that matters most is ASK — in a private deal, price is not set at the bidding stage. It is set by what a buyer learns about a stale NAV while questioning the seller.

01
DISCOVER

Does a tradeable position exist, and who might want it?

02
QUALIFY

Is this counterparty allowed to see the deal at all?

03
DILIGENCE

What is actually being bought?

04
ASK

What is still unknown — and who else needs to know it?

05
COMPETE

What is it worth, and on what terms?

06
CLOSE

Can it actually complete?

Why ASK is a stage and not a feature

Folding questions into diligence hides the step where value is really established — and hides the fairness rule that every answer goes to every qualified buyer at once. If one bidder learns something material in a private email, the process becomes indefensible.

Lane three

Atlas journey — the operating model

Atlas is the only participant present in all six stages. That is the commercial argument for a venue existing at all.

01 · DISCOVERVerify

Reconcile the capital account statement, review the LPA and side letters, confirm the GP contact. A badge is granted only after this.

02 · QUALIFYQualify and match

Score mandate fit, gate access on KYC and NDA, and release identity progressively rather than all at once.

03 · DILIGENCEManage diligence

Track document-level activity, so the seller can see who is genuinely working the deal and who is fishing.

04 · ASKBroker Q&A

Enforce simultaneous disclosure to every qualified buyer and keep an attributable record of every answer.

05 · COMPETERun the process

Sealed rounds, normalised bids, and a certainty score — so a 93% bid with deferred consideration does not beat a 92.5% bid paid at close by default.

06 · CLOSECompliance, GP and settlement

KYC/AML, GP consent and the ROFR clock, documentation, escrow settlement.

The part most concepts leave out

Four ways a process legitimately ends

In this asset class a meaningful share of processes do not close. A journey that only shows the happy path is not describing the market — it is describing a mock-up.

NO · STOPTransferability fails

A blocking restriction in the LPA prevents the transfer. Atlas stops before marketing and no buyer is ever approached — which protects the seller's confidentiality and Atlas's credibility.

NO · DENY / NO ACCESSThe buyer is not qualified, or will not sign

Excluded at KYC, or held outside the data room until the NDA is countersigned. Identity and fund economics are released progressively, never by default.

NO · OUTNo qualifying bid

Nobody bids, or every bid sits below the seller's expectation. The seller reprices and reopens, or withdraws. An honest pass is recorded and useful signal.

NO · PAUSE / NO · NEXT BIDDERCompliance fails, or the GP declines

A compliance exception freezes the entire process. A GP rejection does not collapse the deal — Atlas reconfirms the next shortlisted bidder and restarts consent, because consent is the most common reason a signed deal dies.

The design principle

Exception paths stop, pause or advance to the next bidder. They never loop back silently and they are never left in someone's inbox.

NON-FAILURE TERMINALS

Three states end the process without anything going wrong: the seller withdraws, no bid qualifies, and the ROFR is exercised by another LP. The prototype shows all three rather than pretending every listing closes.

HARD STOP

State 11 is the only true freeze. A compliance exception halts documentation and settlement entirely — it is not a warning, and progress does not resume until it clears.
